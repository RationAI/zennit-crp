SAE-basis CRP — concept-flipping faithfulness by probe site

ViT-small (12 blocks, embed-dim 384) · relevance recipe cp_lrp_baseline (LXT value-path AttnLRP) · perturbation = zero-ablation.

Does decomposing a ViT prediction with CRP onto a learned sparse-autoencoder (SAE) dictionary give more faithful concept-relevance than the standard axis-aligned basis (one concept = one embedding dimension)? And does the answer depend on where we probe? We compare two probe sites × two concept bases — 4 cases:

Reading each panel. Every figure stacks the 3 dataset-trained models side by side (dSprites, ColoredMNIST, FunnyBirds); within each, one panel per transformer block (0–11). A panel is a concept-flipping curve — we rank that block's detectors by signed LRP relevance for the target class, then cumulatively ablate them:
  • most-relevant-first (MoRF) — ablate the strongest detectors first
  • least-relevant-first (LeRF) — ablate the weakest first
  • y = Δ = target-class prob after ablation ÷ baseline prob; x = fraction of detectors ablated n/N.
  • Bands: dark = 95% bootstrap CI of the mean; faint = ±1 std across images. Dot = sign-crossing n★.
  • Faithful ranking ⇒ red (MoRF) drops below green (LeRF): removing the relevance-ranked detectors first collapses the prediction faster. The bigger the red–green gap (area between curves = AOPCmost−AOPCleast), the more concentrated and faithful the relevance.

The 4 cases

What the figures show

The SAE basis is faithful, most clearly on the residual stream. On ColoredMNIST and FunnyBirds the SAE-residual panels show a clean, wide MoRF–LeRF gap across mid/late blocks (red collapses well below green) — relevance concentrates in a small set of SAE latents whose ablation drives the prediction down fast. The axis-aligned baselines at both sites are also faithful but with generally narrower gaps.

dSprites is the flat case (only 3 shape classes, very sparse codes), so its curves stay near Δ=1 with wide CI bands — little to flip. FunnyBirds SAE-residual drew fewer images (the residual SAE's reconstruction error knocked some images below the correctly-classified bar), so its bands are wider there.

SAE reconstruction quality

All 72 SAEs (2 sites × 3 datasets × 12 blocks, α=8, L1) reconstruct faithfully: FVU 0.005–0.044, sparse codes (L0 ~10–270), ~0 dead latents. The splice perturbs the forward pass only slightly (per-image recon error ~8%), and Δ is measured relative to the spliced baseline, so the residual cancels.

Caveat — relevance conservation onto SAE latents. These flipping curves are ranking-based (rank latents by relevance, then ablate), and ranking is scale-invariant, so the curves are valid as shown. However, the strict CRP conservation property — Σi R(latenti) ≈ R(probe) — currently holds only weakly (≈2% of probe relevance lands on the latents). The folded decoder bias equals the mean activation (large), and the γ-rule routes most relevance into that constant bias rather than the latents. The discriminative signal lives in the variable feature part (not the constant mean-offset), so this does not affect the faithfulness ranking — but a clean conservation claim would need bias-aware handling on the decoder (bias-free rule, or treating the mean offset as a skip).

SAE dimensionality sweep

The 4-case study above fixed the SAE at one over-complete size (m=3072, α=8). Here we sweep the dictionary size and ask: how does concept-flipping faithfulness depend on how many SAE latents we decompose onto? Same protocol, same recipe (cp_lrp_baseline), same two probe sites — proj_drop (attention-branch write, before the residual add) and residual (block output, after the residual add). Two toy datasets: dSprites and FunnyBirds.

We compare five concept bases, selectable below:

  • no SAE — the axis-aligned baseline (one concept = one embedding dim, N=384).
  • SAE m∈{154, 384, 768, 1536} — 5% / 12.5% / 25% / 50% of the original m=3072. m=384 is exactly complete; m=154 is undercomplete (fewer latents than dims).

Each SAE is a vanilla L1 autoencoder spliced as a reconstruction pass-through; relevance is decomposed onto its latents f:

f = ReLU(Wenc(a−bdec)+benc)  ·  â = Wdecf + bdec ≈ a.   Rank latents by signed conditional relevance, cumulatively ablate MoRF/LeRF, track Δ(n)=p′c/pc.

How to read the sweep. Curves use the normalised x-axis n/N, so they are on a common scale. But Δ is measured relative to each variant's own spliced baseline, and smaller m reconstructs less faithfully (higher FVU), so absolute Δ curves are not directly comparable across sizes — the integral metric (area between MoRF and LeRF = AOPCmost−AOPCleast) is internally relative and is the right cross-size comparison. ImageNet — ViT-B/16 (pretrained)

Does the toy-dataset story hold on a real, large-scale model? We repeat the sweep on a stock ImageNet-1k-pretrained vit_base_patch16_224 (d=768, 12 blocks, 86 % top-1 on the eval subset) over 10 classes. The toy results predicted: avoid undercomplete; faithfulness grows with m at the late blocks — so the interesting range is complete→over-complete. We sweep m∈{384, 768, 1536, 3072} (0.5× / 1× / 2× / 4× of d) vs no-SAE (axis-aligned, 768).

Verdict. The toy story transfers. On the residual stream the axis-aligned basis is already strong and smooth across blocks; only the larger SAEs (m=1536/3072) match or exceed it, and only at the late blocks, while the undercomplete m=384 is clearly weakest. On proj_drop all bases are flat early and rise sharply at blocks 9–11, where the largest SAE (m=3072) edges ahead. Best dimensionality ≈ 2–4× over-complete (m=1536–3072); undercomplete hurts. (Block-0 residual with large m is degenerate — few correctly-classified spliced images make its Δ-ratio unstable — and is clipped in the metric plot.)
